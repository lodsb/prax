# The review of 2026-11-06: the page split, forwarding, the nightly fixes

Two reviews of the work since 0a6d923: fcb3c95 (a page split by the
door, R3a), eea41c2 (a link to a replaced document finds the current
one), 0da6ba4 (the figures slice passes failed documents by), 748d18e
(the nightly fixes slice, `schedule: fixes`), dd8e2dc (four complex
functions split) and e6309c2 (CI's runner pinned). The safety and
correctness review came first, then the quality review with the
project's skill in quick mode. Each was read by an agent that had not
written the code; every finding was checked again before it was kept.

## Safety and correctness

Found and fixed on the user's word of the same night, each with a test
that fails on the code before:

1. **The fixes slice asked every night for a reading that ran and
   changed nothing.** It passed by a document whose last reading had
   failed, and asked again for one that finished `kept`, `empty` or
   `same`: a marker read that came out shorter than a scan's OCR layer
   leaves the text, its `ocr-layer` finding still names marker, and the
   clock's requests are not forced. Every night the same scan would have
   been read on the card again, and a few dozen of them fill the slice.
   The test itself asserted the re-ask. `store.tried_on_this_text`
   (was `failed_on_this_text`) names the documents whose last reading of
   an extractor failed or changed nothing (`readings.UNCHANGED`) on the
   text they hold; both nightly slices pass them by.
   `tests/test_schedule.py::test_the_fixes_the_checks_name_are_asked_for_a_slice_a_night`,
   `::test_the_figures_backlog_is_asked_for_a_slice_at_a_time`.
2. **Two documents retired into each other sent the page back and
   forth.** `current_of` stopped at a loop but returned the document
   before it, so A forwarded to B and B to A, and `forwardTo` ignored
   `from`. A chain that comes back on itself now has no current copy;
   `retire_document` refuses a `duplicate_of` retired into the document,
   near or far (400 at `POST /doc/{id}/retire`); and a page reached by
   forwarding never forwards again.
   `tests/test_projects.py::test_a_loop_of_replacements_ends`, the
   `forwardTo` case in `tests/ui/lib.test.js`.
3. **A split whose child slug passed 80 characters was always refused**,
   with "the split pages do not add up". The child's slug was never cut
   to `slugs.LIMIT`, `_write_page` cut it and made a second page, and the
   first was left empty. The slug is now `slugs.tagged` of the page's
   and the heading's, and `_free_slug` cuts before its number, so the
   plan names the slug the page is written under.
   `tests/test_page_split.py::test_a_long_slug_is_cut_and_the_page_written_under_it`.

Accepted, not fixed:

4. **A split tells a contributor that a hidden document's title
   exists.** A child's title that a hidden document has comes back with
   " (2)", from a dry run too. The title must differ from every
   document's, because a page's node in the graph is named by its title:
   a child named as a hidden document would be its node. The review of
   2026-11-03 accepted the same channel for slugs, as the cost of unique
   names.

Looked at and found sound:

- `POST /page/{slug}/split` is in `CONTRIBUTOR_ROUTES` only and
  `GET /doc/{id}/cited` in `READER_ROUTES`, both walked by
  `tests/test_wall.py`; `POST /fixes` is the administrator's.
- A split refuses a hidden page as a missing one, joins the children to
  the projects the splitter sees, and gives them the page's sensitivity
  and its decider; a rule's suspicion stays a rule's (`meta.sensitivity`
  keeps `by`).
- `cited_place` and `current_of` show no document the viewer may not
  see.
- A split is one transaction; its chunks keep their ids and vectors, and
  its edges end and are never deleted.
- The four splits of dd8e2dc do what the functions did, compared line by
  line.

## Quality

All four fixed the same night:

1. **The choice of a fix was written in three places.** The clock's
   slice merged two fixes of one reading inline, as `quality.fixes`
   did, and grouped and asked as the bulk route did. `quality.stronger`
   is the merge; `api.passes.request_fixes` and `request_groups` are the
   asking, which the slice and `POST /readings/bulk` both call.
2. **The query of a document's current findings had four copies**, and
   `finding_fixes` had lost the retired filter. `_CURRENT_QUALITY` is in
   `documents.readings` now, and `finding_fixes(ids=None, check=None)`
   is the one reader, `findings_with_fixes` gone. The two filters of
   `select_for_reading` that ask the same inside a subquery keep their
   own SQL.
3. **The split knew how the chunk writer parks rows.** It moved chunks
   to seq far below `_write_chunks`'s -1, -2…, kept apart by a comment.
   `documents.text._adopt_chunks` owns the handover beside the writer.
4. **A split read every child's text back from the archive under the
   write lock**, twice. It compares each child's stored hash with the
   text it wrote, and finds the links in the texts it holds: a split of
   150 sections took 2.05 s and takes 1.18 s on the desktop, 60 sections
   0.87 s and 0.61 s.

Not covered: the UI's citation-follow path in a browser, timings on the
board, and e6309c2 beyond its diff. `ontology.genres()`'s stamp costs
about 0.5 ms a `document_field`, every page write's; it predates the
range and is the next review's.
