# Genres and subjects by the local model, 2026-09-29

Stage Z, step 2 (`docs/PLAN.md`, "What a document is, and what it is
about"). Two questions. How well does the local model (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B
on llama-server, the extraction model) say what a document is and what it
is about, against the user's own labels? And which way of asking is worth
running over the library?

Run with `scripts/eval_genres.py` (read-only on the store; answers in the
scratchpad). The vocabularies are `ontology/genres.yaml` version 3 and
`ontology/subjects.yaml` version 2.

## The gold

78 documents the user labelled on the Review page's "genre" tab.

- **Blind, 30**: labelled with nothing ticked in front of them. This is
  the measure.
- **Checked, 48**: Claude's labels, confirmed or changed by the user.
  They lean towards what was ticked already: on 40 of them the user and
  Claude agreed 39 times, where blind they agree about 6 times in 10. They
  are reported apart, as a second view, not as the measure.

Claude's own labels on the blind 30, given without seeing the user's,
are scored the same way. They are a second reader: what a careful
labeller other than the user reaches.

Scores are per label, micro-averaged: precision, recall, F1. "Genres"
and "subjects" are the labels under a level; "levels" and "groups" are
the eight genre levels (informational, opinion, …) and the four subject
groups (society, technology, …). A level comes with any label under it.

## The methods

- **yes/no**: one question a label ("Is this document a datasheet, that
  is: …?"), P(yes) read from the answer token's alternatives. The levels
  are asked first, and the labels under a level only where the level is
  at least 0.2 likely. The document comes before the question, so
  llama-server reuses its prefix and a further label costs a few tokens.
  Calibrated by a Platt map per facet and kind (level or label), fitted
  on four fifths of the documents and applied to the fifth.
- **list**: the labels as one comma-separated list under a grammar that
  allows only the vocabulary. One call, no probabilities.
- **listed, then asked**: the list proposes, and only the proposed labels
  and their levels are asked. A label is kept when its calibrated
  probability is at least 0.3. Letting in a label the list left out when
  the full yes/no gave it 0.8 or more changed nothing, so it is left out.

The two views: the summary alone (title, source, language, summary,
section summaries), and the same with the first 2,000 characters of the
text.

## What it says

1. **The local model is close to a second reader.** Listed, then asked,
   on the summary and opening: genres F1 0.60 blind against Claude's
   0.64, subjects 0.58 against 0.64. The user and Claude agree no better
   than F1 0.6 to 0.65 on genres; that is the ceiling this task has.
2. **Listing and then asking is the best and the cheapest.** The list
   alone has the recall and not the precision (0.51 on blind genres);
   asking alone has the precision (0.67) and misses labels. The list
   proposing and the question deciding keeps most of both, and asks a
   handful of questions where the top-down yes/no asked about 30.
3. **The opening helps the genres.** 0.58 to 0.60 blind, 0.57 to 0.69
   checked: the form of a text shows in its first lines. The subjects do
   not need it.
4. **The calibration works.** The expected calibration error of the
   yes/no answers falls from 0.07 to 0.01–0.016 out of fold. The raw
   answers are overconfident: every fitted slope is under one.
5. **The levels and groups are easier than the labels.** Groups 0.81
   blind and 0.95 checked; levels 0.72 and 0.83. A domain rule that keys
   on a level or a sharp genre (paper, datasheet, recipe, invoice) rests
   on the better numbers.

30 blind documents are few: a difference of 0.05 between two rows is
within what another 30 could reverse. Three things besides decide it.
The checked set agrees, it is the cheapest of the local methods, and it
is what the literature points to (doc 13331: a model listing labels
suppresses all but one at each step).

## Cost

Seconds a document on the 4090, two slots:

| | summary | summary + opening |
|---|---|---|
| yes/no, top-down | 5.3 | 6.7 |
| list | 0.6 | 1.0 |
| listed, then asked | about 2 | about 2.5 |

Listed, then asked is about 9 hours for the 12,400 open documents.

## The calibration the step reads

Fitted on all 78 documents, summary + opening, as `(a, b)` of
`σ(a·logit(p) + b)`:

```yaml
steps:
  genres:
    model: server-35b
    keep: 0.3
    platt:
      genres levels: [0.4961, -0.7463]
      genres labels: [0.426, -0.8421]
      subjects levels: [0.4041, 1.1441]
      subjects labels: [0.3393, -0.3869]
```

The step (`prax.steps.writing.Genres`, `writing.genres.label`) is built
and off. It runs when `steps.genres.model` names a model and a worker is
asked for it (`prax work --steps genres`). It labels only documents
nobody has labelled, the newest first, stores the model's labels with
their probabilities and the run (`meta.genres_run`), and never writes
over a person's.

## The domain rules, a dry run

`POST /domains/dry-run` counts what a rule set would give the documents
without a domain, and writes nothing. It was run on the live store on
2026-09-29 with one rule a module on the documents' own facts
(`facts:`). A module must hold at least half of the entities a
document's facts name, and there must be five of them at least:

| rule | documents | what they are |
|---|---|---|
| `facts: studio` | 206 | manuals, microphones, audio interfaces, the EM172 builds; one laser-manufacturing news item that is not |
| `facts: research` | 72 | papers, and prax's own notes |
| `facts: kitchen` | 2 | two music albums the extraction read as food: wrong |
| `facts: electronics`, `facts: workshop` | 0 | |
| none | 2,473 | no facts yet, or no module holding half |

Without the minimum of five, the kitchen rule also took a CV and a test
file on two and four facts; the minimum is `RULE_FACTS_MIN`, and a rule
may set its own (`min:`).

The facts are a supplement, not the answer: 2,473 of the documents without
a domain have too few facts to be placed. The genre rules
(`genre: datasheet`, `genre: recipe`, `genre: paper`) are what places
them, and they have nothing to act on until the genres step has run.

## The decision models (the side-quest)

Two open models take Jev's request format (library docs 10307, 13341,
13342). Each was served from its own scratch environment on the 4090,
with llama-server unloaded, and measured with `--decision`. The document
is the `state`, and every label a `noul` question in one request,
calibrated out of fold like the yes/no answers. Summary + opening:

| blind, 30 documents | genres | levels | subjects | groups | s a doc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| local, listed then asked | 0.59 / 0.61 / **0.60** | 0.72 / 0.72 / **0.72** | 0.67 / 0.51 / **0.58** | 0.68 / 1.00 / **0.81** | 2.5–3 |
| Kev-4B, calibrated | 0.77 / 0.45 / **0.57** | 0.75 / 0.70 / **0.73** | 0.67 / 0.48 / **0.56** | 0.66 / 0.90 / **0.76** | 0.5 |
| Jeff 0.8B, calibrated | 0.61 / 0.29 / **0.39** | 0.61 / 0.40 / **0.49** | 0.57 / 0.38 / **0.46** | 0.80 / 0.95 / **0.87** | 1.5 |
| Claude, blind | 0.68 / 0.61 / **0.64** | 0.71 / 0.51 / **0.59** | 0.74 / 0.56 / **0.64** | 0.64 / 0.86 / **0.73** | |

On the checked 48, Kev-4B reaches 0.62 on genres, 0.78 on levels and
0.66 on subjects; the local method 0.69, 0.83 and 0.78.

- **Kev-4B is the local method's equal, at a fifth of the time.** F1
  within a few points, the highest genre precision of any reader
  (0.77), and the best calibrated (Brier 0.042 against the local model's
  0.072). All 60 labels of a document in one request: about 1.7 hours
  for the 12,400 open documents where the local method takes 11.
- **Its cost is the card.** It took 15.7 GB of the 4090 in bfloat16,
  without the fused kernels (they need `flash-linear-attention`, which
  was not installed). It cannot sit beside llama-server's 20 GB. It would
  be a role of its own in the card's swap group, with a PyTorch runtime
  prax does not have.
- **Jeff 0.8B is not enough for genres.** Raw it says yes to nearly every
  label (recall 0.89, precision 0.15), and calibrated it keeps too few
  (0.29). It is good at the four groups (0.87).
- Neither was fine-tuned on these labels. Kev's README shows how
  (`kev.train --init_from`), and 150 labels is the size it expects.
