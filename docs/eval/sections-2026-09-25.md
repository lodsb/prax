# What a book's chapters are worth to a search

2026-09-25. The `sections` pass has read **2,050 long documents** and
written about 10,400 summaries, one per heading region over 4,000
characters. They reach retrieval through the document field
(`_section_lines`, capped at 3,000 characters a document). Nothing had
measured whether that helps.

An A/B on a copy of the store, never the live one: the same questions
against the same chunks, with the section lines in the field and without
them, the field rebuilt both ways for the 2,050 documents.

## The answer

Queries are ten words taken verbatim from the middle of a section. The
field never holds that text — only a generated sentence about it — so a
hit means the summary carried the passage's sense into the field, which
is the whole claim. Document-field BM25, 300 questions, depth 10:

| | hit@10 | MRR |
|---|---|---|
| without sections | 0.087 | 0.048 |
| **with sections** | **0.130** | **0.077** |
| | +0.043 | +0.029 |

Half again as many documents found, and found higher. From a low base —
nine in a hundred to thirteen — but the base is what matters: these are
questions about the *middle* of a 400-page book, which the document field
could say nothing about before, because a book's own summary describes
its first chapter and nothing else.

Modest and real. It does not replace chunk search, which is what finds a
passage; it makes the document field able to say the book is relevant at
all.

## Two designs that were wrong, and why the spread is the point

The first run asked with **section headings** and reported **+0.263**.
That is not a measurement. `_section_lines` writes the heading *and* the
summary into the field, so a heading query matches itself. Six times the
real effect, from a test that could only ever pass.

The second corrected it by removing every word the summary uses from the
query, and reported **+0.007** — nothing. That is the opposite error:
the words excluded are exactly the ones that could match, so the test was
built to fail.

    heading queries                +0.263   circular
    words the summary avoids       +0.007   deflated by construction
    the passage, verbatim          +0.043   the answer

A factor of forty between the two wrong designs. The lesson is not that
one was careless: both are plausible-looking A/Bs, and each was written
to fix the fault in the one before. What separates the third is that the
query comes from the document and the field comes from the model, with
no path between them except the summary — which is the mechanism under
test.

## What this does not measure

Only the keyword half of the document field. Section summaries are also
embedded into `document_embeddings`, and the vector side is not in this
number; a query that paraphrases a chapter rather than quoting it should
do better there, and that wants its own run.

Nor does it measure `ask`. A cheaper way into a long book than its chunks
was the second reason for the pass, and `scripts/eval_ask.py` is the
instrument for it.

## Cost

About sixteen hours of the local model over two days, nothing spent, on
a queue that was reaped twice for memory before it was started detached
(`docs/howto.md`). Three documents needed the pass fixed before they
would read at all: 12,000 characters of Arabic is 23,834 tokens against a
16,384-token slot, and the read budget was in characters.
